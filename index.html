The Line Integrity Method

Four stages. Structure first.

A fence usually fails at a post, a footing, or a connection, then shows up as a leaning run or a broken board. This is how we approach fence, gate, and welding jobs.

01

Assess

Walk the line. Check posts at grade, hardware, and how the failure started, not only the board or weld you can see first.

02

Repair

Fix or replace the failed post, panel, gate, or weld so the opening or run works again.

03

Reinforce

Look at the posts and connections on either side. Strengthen them when they are taking extra load from the failure.

04

Document

When you ask for it, we can send photos of the completed work for a property file, along with the invoice.

Likely issue: the post or footing

A leaning post is often a structure problem, not only a board problem. Replacing a panel on a moving post usually fails again.

Next step. Photograph the post where it meets the ground, plus a wide shot of the run. Then start a fence-repair quote.

Likely issue: the panel, with a post check

Broken boards can be a local repair. We still check the posts and rails, because a split panel is sometimes the first visible sign of movement in the line.

Next step. Photograph the damaged boards and a wider shot of the posts on either side.

Start this quote

Likely issue: hardware, the latch post, or both

A sagging gate often starts at hinges, a dropped post, or a frame that needs welding. The repair depends on which of those has moved.

Next step. Photograph both sides of the opening, the hinges, and the latch post.

Start this quote

Likely issue: several points along the line

After wind or snow load, walk the full run. One obvious break is often next to a post that has also moved.

Next step. Take a wide shot of the line and close shots of each failure. Use weekday timing. This is the winter storm-damage path, not a weekend visit.
